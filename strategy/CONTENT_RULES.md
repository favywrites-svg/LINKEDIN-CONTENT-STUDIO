# Editorial and Content Rules

## Non-negotiable principles

1. Demonstrate expertise and authority, not just awareness of news.
2. Research informs the author's perspective; it does not replace it.
3. Never invent personal experiences, opinions, projects, client results, credentials, or metrics.
4. Never publish or approve content automatically.
5. Do not sacrifice accuracy or usefulness to meet the posting target.
6. Make the business or human problem clear before discussing a tool.
7. Be honest about uncertainty, limitations, and implementation effort.

## What every post must demonstrate

At least one of:
- Understanding of a real problem.
- Knowledge of AI implementation.
- Critical thinking about technology.
- Ability to evaluate business processes.
- Practical understanding of how technology may improve work.

Before drafting, identify:
- Central idea.
- Problem or opportunity addressed.
- Expertise demonstrated.
- Useful perspective contributed.
- Intended reader.

If any of these are unclear, return to ideation or ask the author for input.

## News and industry developments

Do not write posts that merely repeat a headline, announce a product release, or summarize an article.

For a development-based post, explain:
- What happened, with a verified source and date.
- Why it matters to a business, professional, or workflow.
- What it changes and what it does not change.
- Practical adoption considerations, risks, or limitations.
- The author's distinct analysis or question.

A news item without a meaningful interpretation is not automatically a content opportunity.

## Personal experience and opinions

- Ask the author before using a personal story or personal opinion that has not already been supplied.
- Do not infer that the author used a tool simply because it appears in a project plan.
- Do not turn hypothetical examples into claims of real implementation.
- Label illustrative scenarios as hypothetical.
- Keep author-provided facts separate from AI-generated suggestions.

## Project and implementation claims

Use precise status language:
- Concept: an idea not yet built.
- Prototype: a working early version, with limitations.
- Experiment: something tested in a defined context.
- Production: only when the author confirms it is deployed and used operationally.

Do not claim measurable outcomes unless the author supplies evidence and approves the wording.

## Accuracy and sourcing

- Verify factual claims against original or authoritative sources where possible.
- Store source URL, publisher, publication date, and access date.
- Attribute non-obvious facts and statistics.
- Do not cite a source that was not actually accessed.
- Flag unsupported, disputed, outdated, or context-dependent claims.
- Never fabricate citations, quotes, statistics, or source details.
- Distinguish sourced facts, reasonable interpretation, and the author's opinion.

## Visuals

- A post may be published without an image.
- Recommend a visual only if it improves comprehension, makes a complex process clearer, or adds meaningful context.
- Prefer a relevant, reliable, properly attributed source image when appropriate.
- Do not use random stock imagery just to fill space.
- Explain the reason for the visual recommendation.
- Do not assume image-generation capability is available.

## Repetition and originality

- Check previous ideas and posts for repeated angles, examples, and hooks.
- Revisit a topic only when there is a new development, deeper insight, or different useful angle.
- Do not paraphrase another creator's post as original thought leadership.
- Avoid copying distinctive phrasing from sources.

## Approval

A draft may move to **Ready for Review** after quality checks. Only the user can move it to **Approved**.

Approved means the user has reviewed the content and considers it ready to publish. The app does not publish it.

The user may manually mark it **Published** after posting on LinkedIn.
