# Content Strategy

## 1. Professional positioning

Present the author as an AI implementation professional who understands business operations and can identify where technology can solve real problems.

The author brings a pharmacy background and a distinctive perspective on healthcare, digital health, and pharmaceutical technology. This is a useful differentiator, not a restriction: content should also address broader business operations and practical AI implementation.

The content should demonstrate the ability to:
- Understand how work actually happens.
- Identify friction, repetitive tasks, and overlooked inefficiencies.
- Evaluate whether AI or automation is appropriate.
- Think through implementation, limitations, and human impact.
- Translate technical possibilities into practical business outcomes.

## 2. Objectives

1. Attract clients who need AI automation or implementation support.
2. Improve visibility with recruiters and hiring managers for AI automation, AI implementation, and related roles.
3. Build professional credibility and authority.
4. Build relevant professional connections.

## 3. Target audiences

- Recruiters and hiring managers.
- Business owners and founders.
- Operations managers and process leaders.
- Health-tech and digital-health companies.

Write for an intelligent professional audience. Explain technical concepts clearly without assuming every reader is technical.

## 4. Central theme

**AI implementation, business processes, and the human side of technology.**

The content should not simply celebrate new tools. It should examine the problem, the suitability of the technology, the practical work required, and the effect on people and organizations.

## 5. Content pillars and monthly allocation

| Pillar | Target/month | Example angles |
|---|---:|---|
| Practical business problems and AI implementation | 4 | Manual processes, bottlenecks, missed handoffs, repetitive work, deciding what to automate |
| Project builds and workflow demonstrations | 3 | A workflow built, design choices, integration challenges, testing, limitations, lessons |
| AI industry commentary and business developments | 3 | What a development changes for businesses, what it does not solve, adoption implications |
| Human reflections and professional perspectives | 2 | Work, productivity, technology's effect on people, considered personal observations |

Total target: 12 posts per month, approximately 3 per week.

Digital health and pharmaceutical technology should appear naturally across the pillars. They are recurring themes, not a separate mandatory pillar.

## 6. Topic areas

Prioritize:
- AI implementation and identifying where AI is useful.
- AI-powered business process automation.
- AI in healthcare and pharmaceutical technology.
- Healthcare operations and digital-health workflows.
- Business operations, task management, and process optimization.
- Marketing, content creation, and business growth where there is a useful process or implementation insight.
- AI agents and agentic workflows in moderation.

## 7. Editorial balance

Maintain a balance between:
- **Thought leadership:** interpretation, analysis, critical thinking, business implications, and useful opinions.
- **Practical implementation:** workflows, project builds, examples, technical decisions, testing, and trade-offs.

Research should inform the author's perspective, not replace it.

## 8. Research inputs

Use a combination of:
- Relevant AI and technology developments.
- Real business and operational problems.
- The author's ongoing projects and experiments.
- The author's own observations and experiences.

Research should prioritize primary sources, official documentation, original research, research institutions, and credible publications. A source list is maintained in `agents/RESEARCH_GUIDELINES.md`.

## 9. Author contribution

When a topic requires a personal experience, personal opinion, or claim about work the author has done, ask the author for input before drafting.

For unfamiliar topics, the system may propose an angle and ask the author what they think. It must not fabricate a personal perspective.

## 10. Desired audience response

A reader should plausibly think:
- "I should speak with this person about implementing AI."
- "I hadn't considered that AI could help with this problem."
- "This person understands both the business problem and the practical solution."

Content should expose overlooked problems and help readers connect with the person behind the work.

## 11. Content opportunity test

Before an idea is accepted, the system should be able to state:
1. What problem, opportunity, or meaningful development is being discussed?
2. Who should care?
3. What is the central angle?
4. What expertise does the post demonstrate?
5. What useful perspective does the author add?
6. What evidence or author input is needed?

If these questions cannot be answered, the idea is not ready for drafting.
