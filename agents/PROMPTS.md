# Reusable Agent Prompts

Use these prompts as task templates. Inject relevant records and the strategy documents at runtime. Do not assume these prompts alone replace the source-of-truth documents.

## 1. Weekly research

You are researching content opportunities for the author's LinkedIn strategy.

Research a balanced selection of:
- Practical business problems where AI or automation may help.
- AI implementation and business adoption.
- AI in healthcare, digital health, and pharmaceutical technology.
- Relevant developments in AI and agentic workflows.
- Lessons or questions connected to the author's confirmed projects.

Do not produce a news roundup. For each finding:
- State the question investigated.
- Summarize verified facts.
- Provide source URL, publisher, publication date if available, and access date.
- Explain why it matters to a business or professional.
- Identify the underlying problem or opportunity.
- Explain implementation implications and limitations.
- Suggest one or more LinkedIn angles that add analysis.
- Flag uncertainty and missing evidence.

Prioritize primary sources, original research, and credible publications. Do not invent sources or details. If research access is unavailable, say so and offer manual source entry.

## 2. Content idea generation

Using the selected research records and the content strategy, propose distinct LinkedIn ideas.

For each idea provide:
- Working title.
- Content pillar.
- Intended reader.
- Problem/opportunity.
- Central argument or angle.
- Expertise it demonstrates.
- Author perspective or experience required.
- Evidence needed.
- Why it is worth publishing.
- Similarity to prior ideas/posts and how this angle differs.

Reject generic ideas that merely summarize news or repeat common AI enthusiasm. Do not draft the full post yet.

## 3. Author perspective request

This topic may require the author's own experience or opinion.

Briefly explain:
- What the topic is.
- Why the author's perspective would strengthen it.
- The specific information needed.

Ask one focused, conversational question. Do not suggest that the author has had an experience they have not confirmed. Do not draft the post until the necessary input is supplied or the author explicitly chooses a non-personal angle.

## 4. Focused follow-up research

Investigate the factual and technical questions needed for this selected content idea.

Keep the research limited to the central argument. Verify important claims against primary sources where possible. Return source links, dates, evidence, caveats, and any disagreement between sources. Clearly separate verified facts from interpretation.

## 5. LinkedIn post drafting

Draft a LinkedIn post using:
- The selected idea.
- Verified research and source records.
- Author-provided perspective.
- The brand voice and editorial rules.

Requirements:
- Make one central point.
- Start with a specific observation, tension, problem, or claim.
- Explain the reasoning clearly.
- Include a practical implication or useful takeaway.
- Sound conversational, knowledgeable, and human.
- Avoid em dashes, generic AI phrases, hype, fake personal stories, and unsupported results.
- Do not merely report the news.
- Do not force a question or hashtags at the end.
- Do not claim a prototype is a production system.

Return the draft plus a short note listing the sources and any claims that need the author's confirmation.

## 6. Draft revision

Revise the supplied draft according to the author's instructions.

Preserve:
- The central meaning.
- Verified facts.
- Author-provided details.
- The author's intended position.

Improve only what is requested and what is clearly necessary for clarity. Do not add new personal stories, results, or claims. Do not introduce em dashes. Return the complete revised post and briefly state the substantive changes.

## 7. Quality review

Review the draft against the PRD, brand voice, and content rules.

Score or label:
- Factual support.
- Source quality.
- Clarity.
- Audience relevance.
- Originality.
- Practical value.
- Demonstration of expertise.
- Voice consistency.
- Unsupported or exaggerated claims.
- Invented or unconfirmed personal experience.
- Repetition against prior posts.

Return:
1. Overall assessment.
2. Specific issues with quoted short excerpts.
3. Required corrections.
4. Optional improvements.
5. Whether it is suitable to move to Ready for Review.

Do not mark the post Approved. Only the author can approve it.

## 8. Visual recommendation

Decide whether this post needs a visual.

Choose one:
- No visual needed.
- A sourced image would add useful context.
- A simple diagram or workflow illustration would improve understanding.

Explain the reason. If recommending a sourced image, provide its source and usage/attribution considerations. Do not invent an image URL or assume image-generation tools are configured.

## 9. Duplicate and originality check

Compare the proposed idea/draft with the author's previous content records.

Identify repeated:
- Central arguments.
- Examples.
- Hooks.
- Analogies.
- Conclusions.

A topic may recur if the new post adds a genuinely different insight, new evidence, or a new implementation lesson. Explain the difference. Do not claim a comprehensive comparison if prior content records are incomplete.
